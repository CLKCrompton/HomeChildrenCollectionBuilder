## Alfred B. Owen
Alfred de Brissac Owen was heavily involved in the Barnardo Homes organization from 1884 until his quiet removal from the program in Spring 1919. Owen was born in 1863 in Damerham, Hampshire, England and died at 81 on November 13th, 1944, in Kelowna, British-Columbia, Canada. Owen came to Canada in 1882 and began working for Dr. Barnardo’s Homes in 1884, rising from staff member to superintendent in a short period of time. He oversaw Canadian immigration work for Barnardo’s Homes and was responsible for bringing around 25,000 British children into Canada. The Peterborough, Ontario distribution centre, the Toronto, Ontario main office, and the Russel, Manitoba training farm were all initiatives led by Owen. 

Owen’s term as superintendent was not without controversy. In 1910, C. H. Black was appointed as Secretary of the Canadian headquarters of Barnardo’s Homes and began serving directly under Owen. During his employment and even after his resignation in September 1916, Black uncovered Owen’s sinister workings as superintendent of Barnardo’s and attempted to force them to light. In September 1912, Black spoke with a young girl from Barnardo’s Homes who had been impregnated by Owen during her time there. At this time, Owen tried to dissuade Black from investigating by warning him that the home children were unreliable and prone to spreading lies about Owen out of malice. Undeterred, Black continued digging and discovered in 1915 that Owen had been encouraging boys as young as 14 to enlist in the Canadian Overseas Forces. In 1916, Black found payments in the thousands of dollars paid to Owen by men for access to the girls and caused their “moral downfall” (Rose, 1989). Black wrote letters to other members of the Barnardo organization to expose Owen and protect the children. In 1919, Owen was finally arrested for the offence of cohabitating with and impregnating a Barnardo girl, Maisie Skelton, under the guise that she was his housekeeper. Owen paid his bail and was never brought to trial, but the scandal forced Barnardo’s office to move swiftly. A new superintendent, John Hopday, was sent to Canada to quietly take over Owen’s work. Owen, forced to retire, settled on a farm in Kelowna until his death in 1944.
