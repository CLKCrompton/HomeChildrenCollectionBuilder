## Policy Video
embed: <iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/Xyq9qYGYPrQ" allowfullscreen></iframe>

This submission is a video essay (made in Adobe Premiere Pro) that raises the question: how much did Canada’s federal Parliament actually talk about the Home Children program while it was happening? I edited images, narration, Hansard quotes, and screenshots of session papers into the video. The two primary course readings the video draws on in direct ways are: Andrew Doyle’s 1875 Pauper Children report which serves as one of the primary historical pieces that made it obvious the UK was debating placement problems long before Ottawa ever did. Goddard and Seeman’s piece on sustainability helped shape practical choices: I exported a single MP4 video on my own computer with my own software, used a simple static-web embed, and avoided fragile server-dependencies like that of perhaps an ai dubbing or video creation platform so the video remains viewable without the risk of it being taken down in the event of a server crash. The reason this matters is by putting a focused audiovisual argument on the class CollectionBuilder page, while other assignments work towards more of a requirement of deeper knowledge and understanding like having to read a whole essay, this video instead is only 7 minutes long and gets to the necessary points about the Home Children program in a way that’s easy to consume for someone who perhaps might not have the time or desire to read an entire or look through an archival map; which isn’t to say those things are bad or detract from the work we’re doing, the video serves as a simpler accessory that may get people interested in learning more about the subject, which the website provides plenty of sources and options for people to do that research.
