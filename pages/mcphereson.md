## Annie MacPherson
Annie Macpherson was a philanthropist and Scottish evangelical Quaker. She was a pioneer of social welfare, best known for her role in the British Home Children Movement, which  was responsible for sending thousands of British children to Canada. 

Born in 1833, Macpherson grew up in a small parish named Campsie in the city of Stirlingshire, Scotland. She received education in Glasgow and after her move to London in the early 1860s, received education at the Home and Colonial Training College in Gray’s Inn Road. It was here where she began her commitment to missionary work, motivated by her faith after witnessing the harsh conditions in London among the poor. She was particularly distraught within the Matchbox Industry and had an encounter with a starving woman and her child. Macpherson then decided to devote her life to this cause. After donation collecting, she acquired a large old workshop building in 1870 in London and she opened “The Home of Industry”. This was a place where poor children could seek shelter. They would be able to receive basic industrial training while also receiving an education and nourishment. Macpherson thought, however, that sending children overseas for better opportunities was a better idea, so she established homes in Marchmont Gal, Ontario and would later expand in other regions such as Knowlton, Québec. She herself would make the trip multiple times from England to Canada and the first voyage with about 100 boys under her supervision set sail on May 12, 1870. This model set the precedent for subsequent child emigration agencies. The Marchmont Home that she established would be one of the first Canadian receiving homes of its kind. Here, children gathered after being sent from the UK and were then distributed, primarily to rural families to work as farm labourers and domestic servants. This model was replicated by most notably Dr. Barnardo’s Homes for Children. 

Annie Macpherson had a complex legacy. In her time she was viewed as a compassionate and visionary humanitarian that sought to do good in the world as she had seen children starve and suffer in impoverished circumstances. She was responsible for sending over approximately 5,000 children overseas and kickstarted a movement that would send over another 100,000. However from a modern perspective, we can see that while some children received opportunity, countless were faced with hardships, family separation, exploitation and abuse. Macpherson passed in 1904, and her agencies would continue on until the first World War where they would begin to decline due to the dangers of traveling overseas and would completely phase out in the 1920s.
